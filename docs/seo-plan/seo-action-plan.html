1. SEO in plain words

Google works in three steps:

  1. Crawl: Google's robot visits your pages by following links and reading your sitemap.
  2. Index: it stores what each page is about: the text, the headings, the images and the structured data.
  3. Rank: when someone searches, it orders the indexed pages by how relevant, useful and trustworthy they are, and how good the page is to use (speed, mobile friendliness).

For a manufacturer like Ambica Patterns, three areas decide where you appear:

Myths to ignore

"A page needs at least X words"

False. Google has said many times that word count is not a ranking factor. Google's John Mueller: adding more text does not make a page better, and matching a competitor's word count won't make you rank first. Google's Danny Sullivan has said there is no best word count.

What is true: pages that answer a buyer's questions completely tend to be longer, and that completeness is what ranks. So write until the question is fully answered, then stop. Section 6 gives practical length ranges that usually achieve that.

"Repeat the keyword many times"

Keyword stuffing is treated as spam. Use the words buyers use, naturally, in the title, headings and text.

"Meta keywords tag helps"

Google has ignored the meta keywords tag for over a decade. The new site does not use it.

"Buy 1,000 backlinks"

Bought or automated links break Google's spam policies and can get a site demoted or removed. Avoid anyone selling them.

"SEO is a one-time job"

The setup is one-time; results come from small, steady habits: reviews, new content, listings, monthly checks.

3. Launch week: go live without losing anything

Do first Publish

  • Run npm run build and upload the contents of the dist folder to public_html on Hostinger (the README in the project explains each step). Keep a backup of the old files.
  • In hPanel → Emails, create the mailbox website@ambicapatterns.com (the form sends from it). Then submit a test enquiry and confirm it arrives at sales@ambicapatterns.in.
  • In hPanel, purge the CDN cache so visitors see the new site immediately.
  • Check that ambicapatterns.in and www.ambicapatterns.com now redirect to https://ambicapatterns.com. If the .in domain is a separate website in hPanel, add a redirect for it there (hPanel → Domains → Redirects).

Do first Google Search Console (free)

Search Console is Google's own dashboard for your site: what you rank for, how many clicks you get, and any problems it finds.

  1. Go to search.google.com/search-console and sign in with the company Google account.
  2. Add a Domain property for ambicapatterns.com. Google gives you a TXT record; add it in hPanel → DNS Zone. Do the same for ambicapatterns.in so you can see its traffic move over.
  3. Open Sitemaps and submit https://ambicapatterns.com/sitemap.xml.
  4. Use URL Inspection → "Request indexing" for the home page, the products page and the six category pages.
  5. In URL Inspection, click "Test live URL" → "View tested page" → Screenshot. It must show your real page. When we tested the current site with an automated browser, Hostinger's CDN served a "JS challenge" (bot check) page instead of the website. Real Googlebot is normally allowed through, but confirm it here. If Google sees the challenge, turn off bot protection / "under attack" mode in hPanel → Security, or ask Hostinger support to allow verified search engine bots.

Do first Bing Webmaster Tools (free)

Bing also powers several AI assistants. At bing.com/webmasters, choose "Import from Google Search Console": it copies everything in one click.

Next Google Analytics 4 (optional)

Search Console shows Google traffic; Analytics shows what visitors do on the site. Create a GA4 property and paste its ID (G-XXXXXXX) into src/data/site.ts → ga4Id, then rebuild. If you add it, update the privacy policy to mention analytics.

4. Google Business Profile

Ambica Patterns already has a listing on Google Maps ("Ambica Patterns (INDIA) Pvt Ltd"); the job now is to claim it and make it the best in the area.

Google ranks local listings on three things: relevance (does the profile match the search), distance (how close you are to the searcher), and prominence (how well known and well reviewed you are). You cannot change distance, so everything below targets relevance and prominence.

How to use a keyword

Put the main phrase in the page title, the H1 heading and the first paragraph, and use natural variations in the text ("Euro pallet", "Euro-size pallet", "1200 × 800 pallet"). One main topic per page, so two pages don't compete for the same search.

Google's quality guidelines reward content that shows experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust (E-E-A-T). As a manufacturer for 25 years you have something competitors who copy text do not: real knowledge and real photos. Use it.

Ongoing One or two new pages a month

Ideas, roughly in order of value:

  2. Pallet load capacity explained: static, dynamic and racking loads.
  3. How we heat treat pallets: a photo walkthrough of your own chamber and records.
  4. Exporting to Europe: choosing pallets and avoiding rejected shipments.
  5. One-way export pallets: when a Euro-size pallet is enough and when your buyer needs an EPAL pallet.
  6. Wooden vs plastic pallets: honest pros and cons.
  7. How many pallets fit in a 20 ft and 40 ft container.
  8. Protecting pallets from moisture and mould in the monsoon.
  9. Case studies by industry (pharma, auto parts, chemicals), with the customer's permission.
  10. A "Team" or "Our factory" page with names, roles and photos: real people build trust.

8. Links and reputation

A link from another website to yours is like a recommendation. One link from a real, relevant organisation is worth more than thousands from junk sites, and junk links can hurt.

Next Good links to go after

  • Customers and suppliers: many have "partners" or "suppliers" pages. Ask long-standing customers and your timber suppliers to link to you.
  • Associations and chambers you are a member of: member directories usually allow a website link.
  • Trade press: timber, packaging and logistics publications often publish manufacturer news (new capacity, certifications, export milestones). Send a short press note with photos.
  • Exhibitions: exhibitor lists for logistics, warehousing and packaging shows link to exhibitors' websites.
  • Your guides: useful guides (sizes, ISPM-15) get linked naturally by forums, students and buyers. Share them on LinkedIn.

Never do these (they break Google's spam policies)

  • Buying links or "backlink packages", link exchanges, private blog networks.
  • Mass-producing pages with AI or templates just to target keywords ("scaled content abuse").
  • Copying other sites' text or product descriptions.
  • Fake reviews, or keywords stuffed into your business name.

9. Technical health check (30 minutes a month)

  • Search Console → Pages: are pages "Indexed"? Investigate anything under "Not indexed" that should be indexed.
  • Search Console → Core Web Vitals: should say "Good". Targets: page content appears within 2.5 s (LCP), the page responds to taps within 200 ms (INP), and layout doesn't jump (CLS ≤ 0.1).
  • Search Console → Enhancements: Breadcrumbs, FAQ and Product should be valid. Product may show "missing field offers / review": expected, because the site shows no prices. It is a warning, not a penalty.
  • Security & Manual actions: should say "No issues detected".
  • PageSpeed Insights (pagespeed.web.dev): test the home page and one product page on mobile.
  • Never delete or rename a page without adding a 301 redirect in public/.htaccess.
  • New product images: always render them with npm run render (WebP, right size); never upload raw 5–8 MB photos again.

10. AI search

Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, and assistants such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot, increasingly answer questions directly. Google's own 2026 guidance for these features says optimising for them is still SEO: they use the normal Search index and quality systems, and special tricks (llms.txt files, "AI-specific" rewriting, special schema) are not needed for Google.

  • Be the clearest source of facts about your products: specs in tables, plain answers to common questions (already done).
  • Keep company facts identical everywhere (Sections 4–5), because AI assistants cross-check sources.
  • Publish first-hand content competitors can't copy: your process, photos, numbers, case studies.
  • Search Console has a report for visibility in generative AI features; check it monthly once data appears.

14. Glossary

301 redirectA permanent forward from an old address to a new one; passes on ranking value.
BacklinkA link from another website to yours.
CanonicalA tag naming the official address of a page when copies exist.
Core Web VitalsGoogle's speed and stability measures: LCP (loading), INP (responsiveness), CLS (layout stability).
Crawl / indexGoogle visiting your pages / storing them so they can appear in results.
E-E-A-TExperience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust: what Google's quality raters look for.
GBPGoogle Business Profile: your listing on Google Maps and in local results.
Meta title / descriptionThe blue headline and grey text shown for your page in Google results.
NAPName, Address, Phone: must match everywhere.
Schema / structured dataHidden labels that tell Google exactly what a page contains (a product, a business, an FAQ).
SERPSearch engine results page.
SitemapA file listing all your pages for Google: ambicapatterns.com/sitemap.xml.
